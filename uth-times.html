The jump, at a glance

In April the channel had 25,000 followers. By September it had 110,000. That is 85,000 new people choosing to follow, or roughly 14,000 every month, while I worked on it part-time.

The brand already had a large YouTube audience. The opportunity was to bring that energy to short-form social, where a follow has to be earned in seconds.

How each video got made

I took ideas all the way from concept to the final cut, so the brand's voice stayed intact at every step.

  1. Plan the idea

    Pitch topics that young Indians already talk about, shape each one around a single hook, and slot it into a weekly content calendar.

  2. Film it

    Shoot on location and with real people, capturing extra angles and reactions so the edit has room to move.

  3. Cut for retention

    Open on the strongest moment, keep the pace tight, add captions for silent viewing and finish in the brand look.

  4. Publish and learn

    Post on a steady schedule, check what held attention, and feed those lessons into the next round of ideas.

Every video had one job: make a stranger stop scrolling, then give them a reason to follow for the next one.

What made it work

Win the first second

Every video opened on its most surprising line or image. If the first frame did not stop the scroll, nothing after it mattered.

Edit for the rewatch

Tight cuts, no dead air, and an ending that loops or pays off, so people watch to the end and share it on.

Show up every week

A planned calendar kept posts going out on schedule, so new viewers always had a reason to follow for the next one.

Sound like U'th Times

Young, direct and honest. Every piece stayed in the brand's voice, so growth built one recognisable identity.
